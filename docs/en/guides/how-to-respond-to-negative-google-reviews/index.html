How to respond to negative Google reviews

A 1-star or 2-star review is the one you want to answer, and the one that is easiest to get wrong. This is the sequence: wait, name the problem, take it off the listing, do not argue.

Why it matters

The next customer often reads your reply more carefully than the original complaint. A calm, specific answer shows someone is in charge. A long defence confirms the fight.

When you need to respond to a negative Google review, do not type in the rush of service. Read the review twice. Check the diary, the ticket, the shift. If you do not know yet, say so, and give a way to reach you.

Should you respond to negative Google reviews?

Yes. An unanswered 1-star sits on the listing. Google notifies the reviewer when you reply. They can still edit their text. Some do. That is not the goal. The goal is that the next stranger sees a business that answers.

How to respond to a bad Google review

A useful reply has four short parts. Thank them for writing. Name the specific issue in their words (the wait, the bill, the cut). Say you will look at it, or that you already have. Move the next step to a phone, an email, or the counter, with a real name.

Example:

“Hi Pau, thank you for writing. You are right that the wait on Friday was too long. That is not what we want for a first visit. Please call the shop and ask for Marta so we can put it right.”

More templates, including 5-star and mixed, sit in the Google review response examples.

What not to do

Do not contradict the customer in public with “our records show.” Do not blame the staff by name. Do not offer a discount in the reply as a prize for changing the stars. Google already forbids paying to alter a review.

If the review is fake, report it, and still keep the public line short and civil. If it is true, fix the thing in the shop. The listing will not save a repeated mistake.

Reply within a day if you can.
